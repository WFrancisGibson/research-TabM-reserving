# TabM bCCNN reserving

An R port (keras3 1.5.1 on the torch backend) of the TabM framework applied to the bCCNN. 

> **bCCNN.** Gabrielli, A. (2020), *Claims reserving and neural networks*, PhD thesis ETH Zürich,
> Chapter 4 and Paper C (Gabrielli, Richman & Wüthrich 2020, *Neural network embedding of the
> over-dispersed Poisson reserving model*, SAJ). The blended cross-classified network: a
> (20, 15, 10) tanh network started exactly in the chain-ladder (ccODP) model.
>
> **TabM.** Gorishniy, Kotelnikov & Babenko (2025), *TabM: Advancing tabular deep learning with
> parameter-efficient ensembling*, ICLR 2025. k weight-sharing "submodels" trained together in one
> network, prediction = mean of the k outputs.

The mathematics is written out in the Python project's `TabM_bCCNN_explained.md`; its equations
(T1)–(T8), cited in the code, are summarised under [Model notes](#model-notes) (see below). The data is the
thesis's seed-75 simulation (six lines of business, 12 × 12 triangles), a byte-identical copy of the
Python project's CSV.

## The six configurations

| label | model | where |
|---|---|---|
| `single_bccnn` | Paper C (13)/(14), Listing 4: one bCCNN with the trainable skip weight `w` | `R/nn_models.R`, `bccnn_model()` |
| `tabm_tanh_signs` | TabM: k bCCNNs sharing their hidden weights, first adapter `R_1` random ±1 | `R/nn_models.R`, `tabm_model()` |
| `tabm_tanh_normal` | TabM with `R_1 ~ N(0,1)` (TabM's rule for embedding inputs, App. A.2) | `R/nn_models.R`, `tabm_model()` |
| `tabm_relu_signs` | as `tabm_tanh_signs` with the TabM paper's ReLU between layers | `R/nn_models.R`, `tabm_model()` |
| `tabm_relu_normal` | as `tabm_tanh_normal` with ReLU | `R/nn_models.R`, `tabm_model()` |
| `packed_tanh` | deep ensemble: k fully independent bCCNNs trained in lockstep | `R/nn_models.R`, `tabm_model()` |

Each configuration is a `{kind, r1_init, activation}` entry of `configs:` in `config.yml`
(`kind`: `single`, `tabm` or `packed`); their order there is the column order of every table, and
`pretty:` holds their labels in figures and tables.

All ensembles: k = 20 (`tabm: k`), skip connection `alpha_hat_i + beta_hat_j` with weight 1 inside the
exponential (thesis eq. (4.3)), heads initialised at `w = 0, w_0 = c_hat` so every submodel starts in
the ccODP model (T5)–(T6), loss = Keras `poisson` on the observed `y` repeated in k columns (= TabM's
mean of the k individual losses), Keras `rmsprop` defaults, full batch, dropout 10 %.

## Protocol (Paper C Section 3.3, with two validation splits)

1. **Validation split** (`split`, `holdout`). *Time-aware*: the training triangle is the triangle as
   it stood two calendar years before valuation (10 × 10, 55 cells); the validation cells are the
   next two diagonals (17 of the 23 held-out cells; the 6 whose accident or development year has no
   ccODP parameter in the training triangle cannot be predicted and are excluded). *Claim-level*:
   Paper C's 50/50 split of the individual claims, both halves aggregated to 78-cell triangles.
2. **Stage 1** (02). ccODP MLE on the training triangle; every model starts in it; full-batch
   gradient descent for the whole window (`epochs`: 6,000 steps time-aware, 2,000 claim-level);
   after every step the deviance of the collective prediction (and of the individual submodels) on
   the training and the validation cells, dropout off, averaged over 5 seeds (`seeds`).
3. **Stopping rules** (03, `stopping`) on the pooled curve (each LoB normalised by its ccODP start,
   averaged): the smoothed (51-step) **minimum** = the thesis's rule (the "protocol" stop); the slope
   **elbow** (first step after which the next 200 steps gain less than 1 % of the gain so far); the
   paper's fixed **300**.
4. **Stage 2** (04, 05). ccODP MLE on the full 12 × 12 upper triangle; every model started in it and
   refitted for each stop (one run per seed, snapshots at the stops); reserves = sum of the
   lower-triangle collective means; bias against the true reserves; lower-triangle deviance; seed
   spread; submodel diversity.
5. **Bootstrap** (06, `bootstrap`). Parametric ODP bootstrap around the fitted means with the
   reduced dispersion of Paper C Sec. 3.3.2, 25 replicates, one ensemble fit per replicate, at the
   elbow stop by default.

## Model notes

### ccODP model ( `R/fit ODP GLM.R`)

```
E[Y_ij] = mu_ij = exp(c + alpha_i + beta_j),    Var[Y_ij] = phi * mu_ij                 (1)
L = 2 * sum over the observed cells of ( mu - y + y * log(y / mu) ),  y log y = 0 at y = 0   (4)
phi_hat = L / (number of observed cells - number of parameters) = L / (78 - 23) = L / 55     (5)
```

- Corner constraints: alpha and beta of the first accident and development year are 0.
  `glm(y ~ ay + dy, family = quasipoisson())` with R's treatment contrasts is exactly
  that parametrisation; `glm.control(epsilon = 1e-14, maxit = 100)`; the coefficients are taken by
  position (intercept c, then the 11 accident-year and the 11 development-year effects).
- The means over the whole rectangle, `exp(c + alpha_i + beta_j)`, are computed from the
  coefficients, the arithmetic the network's skip connection performs; the reserve is their sum over
  the lower triangle (by accident year and in total).
- (4) is the deviance loss with phi = 1; (5) is the deviance-based dispersion the paper uses
  (`summary(fit)$dispersion` would be the Pearson one).
- The ML fit reproduces the volume-weighted chain ladder exactly under certain conditions: 01 checks
  it with `stopifnot()` against the ChainLadder package, and so does test-ccodp.

### Data (Paper C Listings 2 and 3; `R/triangles.R`)
- A triangle holds the complete rectangle of incremental payments, observed and future (known because
  the data is simulated: the backtest truth, never seen by a fit), and the mask of the cells observed
  at valuation. Every fit goes through `train_cells()`: the observed cells (i, j, y) in row-major
  order, i slowest . The run-off mask is i + j < n with 0-based indices, leading to 78 cells of a
  12 × 12 triangle.
- Listing 2, the 50/50 claims split: order the claims by (LoB, AY) (a stable order, so within an
  accident year the claims keep their simulation order) and assign 1, 2, 1, 2, ... down that order.
  Every (LoB, AY) group splits evenly (off by one when its size is odd). The ccODP parameters are
  volume dependent, so both halves must carry (nearly) equally many claims per LoB and accident year
  for parameters fitted on one to be meaningful on the other.
- Listing 3: aggregate each portfolio to LoB × AY × DY incremental payments (columns `Pay00` ..
  `Pay11`), divide by 1,000 (thousands), relabel accident years 1994–2005 as 0–11. The valuation date
  is the end of 2005, so AY + DY ≤ 2005 is the observed upper triangle D_I of 78 cells per LoB.
- `lob_triangles()` gives per LoB the triangles `full`, `train` and `validation` (the two halves),
  all with the same run-off mask.

### Time-aware split (`time_aware_split()`)
Paper C's validation triangle is the upper triangle of an independent half-portfolio. The time-aware split holds out the
final h calendar years (`holdout: 2`) instead. With 0-based i, j and calendar index t = i + j (the
upper triangle is t ≤ n − 1):

```
training   : t <= n - 1 - h                                   the triangle as it stood h years earlier
validation : n - h <= t <= n - 1, restricted to i, j <= n - 1 - h
```

- The training set is itself a complete run-off triangle over n − h years, so its ccODP MLE gives
  alpha_i and beta_j for every year it contains; the validation cells are the first h diagonals
  below it: an out-of-time backtest of exactly the extrapolation a reserve needs.
- The restriction is forced: a held-out cell whose accident or development year is not in the
  training triangle has no alpha_i or beta_j, and no model can predict it.
- n = 12, h = 2: a 10 × 10 training triangle with 55 cells, 17 validation cells (calendar indices
  10 and 11), and 6 of the 23 held-out cells excluded: (0,10), (0,11), (1,10), (10,0), (10,1), (11,0).
- Both sets are triangles over the 10 × 10 square, so `train_cells()` of the one gives the fitting
  cells and of the other the validation cells.

### Single bCCNN (Paper C (13)/(14), Listing 4; `bccnn_model()`)

```
mu(i,j) = exp( w * (alpha_i + beta_j) + c_D + < B_D, z(i,j) > )        (13)
start:    w = 1,  c_D = c_hat (ccODP intercept),  B_D = 0                (14)
```

- alpha_i and beta_j are the ccODP MLEs, frozen in embedding layers (`AY_embed`, `DY_embed`);
  `CC0` is their sum (the skip connection); `concate0` = (alpha_i, beta_j) feeds three tanh layers
  (20, 15, 10) with dropout 0.1 after each (`hidden1`..`hidden3`, `dropout1`..`dropout3`);
  `Response` is an exponential dense layer on (`CC0`, network output) with kernel (1, 0, ..., 0) and
  bias c_hat.
- Before the first step the network *is* the ccODP model and its reserve the chain-ladder reserve.
  Unlike the ensembles it keeps Paper C's trainable `w`, as Listing 4 does. Hidden layers: Keras'
  glorot_uniform kernels and zero biases.
- Its submodel means are an array (1, n, n), so every configuration goes through the same code.

### TabM-bCCNN, (T1)–(T8) (`tabm_model()`; TabM Sections 3.2–3.3, thesis (4.3)–(4.4), Paper C (10))
For a cell (i, j) and submodel l = 1, ..., k (`*` elementwise):

```
z_0(i,j)     = (alpha_i, beta_j)'                                        frozen ccODP MLEs
z_d^(l)(i,j) = act( c_d^(l) + s_d^(l) * B_d' (r_d^(l) * z_{d-1}^(l)) )   d = 1, 2, 3     (T1)
eta^(l)(i,j) = w_0^(l) + < w^(l), z_3^(l)(i,j) >                         one head per submodel
mu^(l)(i,j)  = exp( alpha_i + beta_j + eta^(l)(i,j) )                    skip connection, (4.3)
mu_bar(i,j)  = (1/k) sum_l mu^(l)(i,j)                                   the TabM prediction
```

- The matrices B_d are shared by all submodels; each submodel owns only its adapter rows r, s, c and
  its head.
- (T2) Submodel l has the effective weight matrix `B_d * outer(r_d^(l), s_d^(l))`: a rank-one
  re-scaling of the shared matrix, not a free matrix.
- (T3) The BatchEnsemble layer (`layer_batch_ensemble`, Keras class `BatchEnsembleLinear`) works on
  tensors with a submodel axis, shape (n, k, q): `Z_d = ((Z_{d-1} * R_d) B_d) * S_d + C_d`, with one
  row of R (k × q_in), S and C (k × q_out) per submodel, broadcast over the batch. A layer that
  multiplies such a tensor by one matrix applies the same B to every submodel, so weight sharing is
  the default. B starts at glorot_uniform, S at ones, C at zeros.
- (T4) The skip connection alpha_i + beta_j enters inside the exponential with weight 1, broadcast
  over the k submodels, without an adapter (thesis (4.3)); Paper C's trainable `w` is used only in the
  single bCCNN.
- (T5) The heads start at w^(l) = 0 and w_0^(l) = c_hat, so (T6) every submodel and the ensemble
  start exactly in the ccODP model, whatever the hidden layers look like.
- (T7) The target is the observed y repeated in k columns: Keras' `poisson` loss, a mean over the last
  axis and then over the batch, is TabM's mean of the k individual losses, and nothing in the loss
  couples the submodels. The batch is the whole triangle, so TabM's shared-batch / own-batch
  distinction (its Sec. 3.4) does not arise.
- (T8) The individual deviance, the mean over l of the deviance of mu^(l), is the scale the training
  objective works on (TabM's "individual" curve); the collective deviance is that of mu_bar.
- `kind: tabm`: (T1) with the very first adapter R_1 random, ±1 (`r1_init: signs`, TabM Sec. 3.2) or
  N(0,1) (`normal`, TabM App. A.2 for embedding inputs, which the ccODP inputs are), and every other
  multiplicative adapter exactly 1 ("TabM = BatchEnsemble + better initialisation"): at the start the
  submodels differ only through R_1. R_1 is drawn from R's RNG (`sample()` / `rnorm()`) inside
  `tabm_model()`, where the first BatchEnsemble layer is built, after `nn_fit()` has called
  `set_random_seed()` 
- `kind: packed`: k fully independent networks (no sharing, no adapters) trained together on the
  same data: the deep ensemble of k bCCNNs that TabM is meant to replace, run in lockstep so that its
  collective validation curve can be tracked too. `layer_packed_linear` (`PackedLinear`) evaluates
  `out[n, l, ] = Z[n, l, ] W[l, , ] + b[l, ]` with one einsum, each kernel drawn from
  U(±sqrt(6 / (q_in + q_out))) like an ordinary dense kernel; it also holds the k heads of every
  ensemble.
- The k copies of z_0: (n, 2) → (n, 1, 2) → (n, k, 2) (`z0_row`, `copy_to_submodels`). Dropout on the
  3-D tensor draws an independent mask for every (cell, submodel, unit), as `nn.Dropout` does in
  Python.
- Trainable parameters: 490 + 138 k for `tabm` (3,250 at k = 20), 546 k for `packed` (10,920) and 547
  for the single bCCNN (the 24 frozen embedding weights not counted).
- The layer names are fixed, because the weights-parity test loads the Python weights by name: `be1`
  .. `be3` (tabm), `packed1` .. `packed3` (packed), `head`.

### Training and curves (Listing 4; `nn_fit()`)
- Full-batch gradient descent: one epoch is one step on all observed cells (batch size = number of
  cells, as Listing 4's batch size |D_I| = 78), no shuffling, Keras RMSprop with lr 0.001, rho 0.9,
  epsilon 1e-7. Keras' `poisson` loss, mean(mu − y log mu), differs from the deviance
  (4) only by terms in y alone and a factor two, so the gradients are proportional.
- The curves (`train_collective`, `train_individual`, `validation_collective`,
  `validation_individual`): entry 1 is the ccODP start (0 steps), entry e + 1 the value after e steps,
  all from one forward pass over the k × n × n rectangle with dropout off (`nn_predict()`), in a
  `callback_lambda()`. Keras' own `loss` (dropout on, before the step) is not used; Keras'
  `poisson` loss on the validation cells serves only as a cross-check (test-tabm-history).
- The same pass stores the submodel means at the requested snapshot steps. Snapshots of one run equal
  separate runs stopped at those steps (same seed, same random stream), so 04 reads all
  three stops off one run per seed.

### Stopping rules (`R/early stopping.R`)
Paper C reads "roughly 300" off a rough dropout curve by eye and uses it for all LoBs. Here every rule
works on the pooled curve: per LoB the collective validation curve averaged over seeds and divided by
its step-0 (ccODP) value, so the six portfolios weigh equally, then averaged over the LoBs. One shared
step count per configuration comes out; every function returns a 0-based step count.

- **Protocol** (the smoothed minimum): the argmin
  of the centred 51-step moving average (`smooth`). The edges where the window does not fit are
  dropped rather than padded, which would bias the minimum outwards. `at_edge()` flags a minimum in
  the last 51 steps of the window: the curve is still descending and the step is an artefact of the
  window.
- **Elbow** : on the time-aware split the curves fall steeply and then drift along a flat plateau, so the minimum sits at the end of whatever window was searched. With s the smoothed curve (edges padded with the first / last interior value, so s keeps the length of the curve), the elbow is the first step t with

  ```
  s(t) - s(t + window) <= tol * (s(0) - s(t))    and    s(0) - s(t) > 0
  ```

  (`elbow_tol: 0.01`, `elbow_window: 200`), or the last step if that never happens. The sensitivity
  table repeats it for every tol in `tols` (0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05).
- **300**: the fixed step count of Paper C Sec. 3.3.3 (`fixed`), capped at the window length.
- The validation decrease at a stop (Paper C Table 3) is, per LoB, 100 (1 − v(t) / v(0)) with v the
  collective validation deviance.

### Bootstrap and reduced dispersion (Paper C (15), (16), Sections 3.3.2 and 3.3.4; `R/bootstrap.R`)
- The conditional RMSEP (15) splits into the process variance phi · R of (16), available in closed
  form (R the reserve), and the parameter estimation error, the standard deviation of the reserves
  refitted on bootstrap replicates:

  ```
  rmsep = sqrt( phi * reserve + sd(bootstrap reserves)^2 )
  ```

- Parametric bootstrap from assumption (1): Y* / phi ~ Poisson(mu / phi), so Y* = phi · Poisson(mu /
  phi), with mean mu and variance phi · mu (Model Assumptions 2.1). Only the upper triangle is
  resampled. Each replicate is refitted end to end: a fresh ccODP on the resampled triangle and from
  it a fresh network trained for the chosen number of steps (for an ensemble, one fit of all k
  submodels).
- ccODP bootstrap: mu and phi of the ccODP fit, seed `bootstrap: seed` + m − 1 for LoB m.
- Network bootstrap: mu is the seed-0 collective mean mu_bar at the chosen stop (`<prefix>_means.rds`)
  and the point reserve its lower-triangle sum. With s = `bootstrap: seed` + 50 + m − 1: since
  `set_random_seed()` also reseeds R's RNG, all replicate triangles are drawn up front after
  `set.seed(s)`; the network of replicate r trains with seed s · 1000 + r − 1; the Keras session is
  cleared every 5 replicates; failed replicates are counted.
- Reduced dispersion (Sec. 3.3.2): the ccODP estimate (5) corrects the in-sample loss by the number of
  estimated parameters, which is meaningless for an early-stopped network with dropout. The paper
  scales phi_ODP down by the relative decrease of the validation loss over the selected number of
  steps, flooring a negative decrease at zero (its LoB 3, where the bCCNN does not improve out of
  sample):

  ```
  phi_NN = phi_ODP * (1 - max(decrease, 0) / 100)
  ```

  on the time-aware split the decrease is that of the collective validation deviance on the
  held-out diagonals at the chosen stop; no validation figure belongs to the fixed 300 steps, so they
  get no reduction.
- The default stop is the elbow.

## Keras 3 in R: what bit

* `KERAS_BACKEND` / `RETICULATE_PYTHON` before keras3 loads: `analysis/00_setup.R` sets them before it
  sources `R/`, because sourcing `R/nn_models.R` loads the keras3 namespace. The `py_require()`
  warning (tensorflow, torchaudio) when Python starts is harmless.
* `keras3::Layer()` is lazy (the Python class is made at its first use), so `R/nn_models.R` defines
  the two custom layers at its top level before `library(keras3)` and before Python starts.
* `callback_lambda()` passes a **1-based** epoch in R (Python Keras: 0-based). `nn_fit()` counts its
  own steps instead of trusting the argument; the first version was one step off.
* `initializer_constant()`, not `set_weights()` after the build (as Masters-Research-R's bCCNN does):
  a constant draws no random numbers, while a default initialiser replaced later would, and every
  later Glorot draw and dropout seed would move.
* `initializer_constant()` broadcasts a bare vector: pass matrices with explicit `dim`, and a scalar
  for a scalar bias (a 1×1 matrix makes a (1,1) variable where Keras declared (1,)).
* Keras' `glorot_uniform` on a 3-D `(k, q_in, q_out)` weight multiplies the fans by k; the packed layer
  therefore draws `U(±sqrt(6/(q_in+q_out)))` explicitly.
* The Keras inputs are an **unnamed** positional list (accident year column, development year column),
  in the order of the model's inputs; it gives the same results as the named list of the first version.
* `%>%` (re-exported by keras3) creates the layers in the same order as `|>`: Keras draws initialiser
  and dropout seeds in construction order, so the pipe changes nothing.
* keras3 R ops take **1-based** axes: `op_repeat(x, k, axis = 2L)` is Python's `axis = 1`.
* `layer_lambda` on a 3-D tensor needs `output_shape`; a length-1 R vector reaches Python as a scalar
  (wrap in `array(x, dim = 1)` before `set_weights`).
* `set_random_seed()` also reseeds R's RNG: bootstrap triangles are drawn before any training.
* The deviance curves come from a `training = FALSE` forward pass in the callback, never from Keras'
  `loss` (dropout on, before the step).
* On the torch backend, `fit(validation_data = )` and `evaluate()` during training draw from torch's
  global RNG, which the (unseeded) dropout masks also use: a same-seed run with validation data
  leaves one without it from step 2 on. `nn_fit()` therefore scores the curves with direct
  `model(x, training = FALSE)` calls, which draw nothing.
* Torch's thread count changes the `packed_tanh` einsum at about 1e-8 relative: bit-identical reruns
  need the same thread setting (`run_all.ps1 -Quick` passes none).
* One full-batch step on 78 cells costs ~33 ms at k = 20 in keras3 (PyTorch: 4 ms); the recorder adds
  ~15 ms; hence the parallel-by-configuration chain.
* PowerShell 5.1 has no `&&`; `Start-Process` joins its `-ArgumentList` without quoting, so
  `run_all.ps1` quotes the script paths (the folders have spaces) and passes `-WorkingDirectory` so
  that `here::here()` finds the project root; `Start-Process` logs are plain text, `*>>` writes UTF-16.
